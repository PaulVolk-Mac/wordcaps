Free auto captions for YouTube Shorts – 4 ways that look like real Shorts

Most people watch Shorts with the sound low or off. The captions that keep them watching are the bold, word-by-word captions burned into the video – and YouTube's own subtitles don't do that. Here are four ways to get them, from free to one click.

1. YouTube's automatic subtitles (free, but hidden)

After upload, YouTube Studio can create automatic subtitles for your Short. They are closed captions: viewers only see them if they turn captions on, and you can't style them. Still worth checking and correcting them in YouTube Studio – they help accessibility and search – but they don't replace on-screen captions.

Cost: 0 €. Look: plain white subtitles, off by default.

2. Free, technical: Whisper + FFmpeg

The open-source speech model Whisper can give you a timestamp for every word (for example faster-whisper with word_timestamps=True). Turn the words into an ASS subtitle file with one event per word, then burn it in: ffmpeg -i short.mp4 -vf ass=captions.ass out.mp4.

Cost: 0 €. Time: an evening of setup the first time, Python knowledge helps.

3. Editor apps with auto captions (CapCut and others)

Phone and desktop editors can auto-caption Shorts in one tap. CapCut moved auto captions into its Pro plan in 2025 for most users; other free editors often add a watermark or limit exports. Check the current limits in the app you use. For CapCut, see CapCut auto captions without Pro.

Tips for Shorts captions

  • 2–3 words on screen read fastest on a phone; one word at a time suits punchy, fast talk.
  • Safe area: the bottom part of a Short is covered by the title and channel name, the right edge by the like/comment icons.
  • Upload the SRT too: burned-in captions for viewers, the SRT as YouTube subtitles for accessibility and search.
  • Highlight keywords: numbers and names in a second colour make the hook easier to catch.

FAQ

Does YouTube add captions to Shorts automatically?

YouTube can create automatic subtitles, but they are closed captions: hidden unless the viewer turns them on, and not styled. That's why most creators burn captions into the video.

What is the best free way to get word-by-word captions?

Whisper + FFmpeg costs nothing but needs some setup. WordCaps Studio does the same in a window for €4.99 once.
