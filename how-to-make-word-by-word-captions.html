How to make word-by-word captions – every way in one guide

Word-by-word captions show only one to three words at a time, exactly when they are spoken – usually with the active word highlighted in a second colour. It is the caption look of TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reels, because it can be read in a split second with the sound off. This page shows every way to make them, depending on the editor you already use.

1. In CapCut

CapCut makes the look with auto captions plus a caption template that highlights the spoken word. Since the 2025 pricing change, auto captions show a Pro prompt for most users. Importing an SRT and styling it as text still works without Pro. Details and free workarounds: CapCut auto captions without Pro – 3 ways · WordCaps Studio vs CapCut.

2. In Premiere Pro

From Premiere 26.3 (June 2026): Window → Text → Transcript, transcribe, Create captions → Single Word. Every word is synced, but all words share one style – no colour on the word being spoken. Older versions use the “7 characters” workaround. Step by step: Word-by-word captions in Premiere Pro.

3. In DaVinci Resolve

Automatic subtitles (Create Subtitles from Audio) are a feature of the paid Resolve Studio. In the free version, import an SRT with one word or a short group per entry (File → Import → Subtitle) or type captions by hand. Step by step: Word-by-word subtitles in DaVinci Resolve (free version).

4. Free in any editor: Whisper + ASS karaoke + FFmpeg

This is how the paid tools do it under the hood. It costs nothing and gives you the highlighted active word (k vs kf explained in Karaoke style captions).

  1. Word timestamps: transcribe with the open-source Whisper model, e.g. faster-whisper with word_timestamps=True. You get start and end time for every word.
  2. Group the words: 1–3 words per caption. Start a new caption at every pause longer than ~0.4 s and after every full stop, so no caption runs across two sentences.
  3. Write an ASS file: one Dialogue line per group, with a \k tag before each word. The number is the word’s duration in centiseconds. Each word is drawn in the style’s SecondaryColour until it is spoken, then in its PrimaryColour: 
    [V4+ Styles]
Format: Name, Fontname, Fontsize, PrimaryColour, SecondaryColour, OutlineColour, BackColour, Bold, Italic, Underline, StrikeOut, ScaleX, ScaleY, Spacing, Angle, BorderStyle, Outline, Shadow, Alignment, MarginL, MarginR, MarginV, Encoding
Style: Cap,Arial,22,&H0000E1FF,&H00FFFFFF,&H00000000,&H00000000,-1,0,0,0,100,100,0,0,1,3,0,2,40,40,80,1

[Events]
Format: Layer, Start, End, Style, Name, MarginL, MarginR, MarginV, Effect, Text
Dialogue: 0,0:00:01.20,0:00:02.05,Cap,,0,0,0,,{\k35}THIS {\k20}IS {\k30}EASY
    Colours in ASS are written as &HAABBGGRR (blue-green-red), so &H0000E1FF is yellow and &H00FFFFFF is white.
  4. Burn it in: ffmpeg -i video.mp4 -vf ass=captions.ass -c:a copy out.mp4. More options (HandBrake, VLC): Burn subtitles into a video for free.

Cost: 0 €. Time: an evening of setup the first time; a small Python script does steps 2–3 for every next video.

Tips that make word-by-word captions readable

  • 1–3 words: one word suits fast, punchy talk; three words feel calmer. More than four is no longer “word by word”.
  • Never split a sentence oddly: break at pauses and full stops, keep “the” and “a” with the next word.
  • Safe area for 9:16: keep captions slightly below the middle – the bottom is covered by the title and channel name, the right edge by the like and comment buttons.
  • Bold font + dark outline (2–4 px at 1080p) reads on every background; avoid thin fonts.
  • Check names and numbers: speech models get them wrong most often, and they are exactly the words people read.
  • Upload the SRT too on YouTube: burned-in captions for viewers, the SRT for accessibility and search.

FAQ

What is the difference between word-by-word and normal subtitles?

Normal subtitles show a full line for a few seconds. Word-by-word captions show 1–3 words, change with every word or two and often highlight the word being spoken. They are made for short vertical videos watched without sound.

Can I make word-by-word captions without an editor subscription?

Yes: way 4 (Whisper + ASS + FFmpeg) is completely free, and WordCaps Studio is a one-time €4.99 purchase. DaVinci Resolve free can show word-level captions from an imported SRT, without a highlight.

Are word-by-word captions possible on a phone?

Mobile editors such as CapCut offer them in the app; check which styles your plan includes. The free and offline methods on this page are for desktop.

Which languages work?

Whisper, which ways 4 and 5 use, recognises around 100 languages; English, German, Spanish and other large languages work best.
