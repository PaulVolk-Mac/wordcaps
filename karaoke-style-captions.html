Karaoke style captions – every word lights up as it is spoken

Karaoke style captions (also called karaoke subtitles) colour each word at the exact moment it is spoken, like lyrics on a karaoke screen. On TikTok, Reels and YouTube Shorts they are usually combined with word-by-word captions: only 1–3 words on screen, the active one in a second colour. The eye always knows where to look, even with the sound off.

CapCut gets the look from auto captions plus a caption template that highlights the spoken word. Since the 2025 pricing change, auto captions show a Pro prompt for most users, and some templates are Pro-only. Free workarounds are on CapCut auto captions without Pro.

Karaoke style captions in Premiere Pro

Premiere Pro can make one-word captions (Single Word option or the short-line workaround), but a caption track has one style – the spoken word is not highlighted on its own. Three ways around it: build each highlight by hand with keyframed text (fine for a 5-second hook), use a captions plugin, or burn karaoke captions into the clip with a separate tool and edit that clip in Premiere.

Karaoke style subtitles in DaVinci Resolve

The subtitle track in Resolve has no active-word highlight either, and automatic subtitles need the paid Studio version. In the free version you can animate Text+ titles by hand in Fusion, or import an SRT (3 ways for the free version). For a real karaoke effect, burn it in first and edit the result.

The free way: ASS karaoke tags + FFmpeg

The ASS subtitle format has had karaoke built in for over 20 years. Every word gets a tag with its duration in centiseconds (1/100 s):

Dialogue: 0,0:00:01.20,0:00:02.40,Caps,,0,0,0,,{\k32}THIS {\k28}IS {\k60}KARAOKE
  • \k – the word switches colour at once when it starts (the punchy TikTok look).
  • \kf (or \K) – the colour fills the word from left to right (classic sing-along look).
  • \ko – only the outline changes.

The word is shown in the style's SecondaryColour before it is spoken and in the PrimaryColour from then on. Steps:

  1. Transcribe with Whisper and turn on word timestamps (for example faster-whisper with word_timestamps=True).
  2. Group the words into phrases of 1–3 words, new phrase at every pause.
  3. Write one Dialogue line per phrase with a \k tag per word (word end − word start, in centiseconds).
  4. Burn it in: ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -vf ass=captions.ass -c:a copy out.mp4

If you would rather time karaoke by hand, the free subtitle editor Aegisub has a karaoke timing mode. More detail on the free method: How to make word-by-word captions · Burn subtitles into a video for free.

Limits: the first setup takes an evening, and a pop-in or bounce animation means writing more ASS tags by hand.

Tips for karaoke captions that people actually read

  • 1–3 words per line on vertical video; whole sentences make the highlight jump too far.
  • Strong contrast: white or light grey for upcoming words, one bright colour for the spoken word, a dark outline for both.
  • Keep clear of the app buttons: roughly the middle of the 9:16 frame, away from the bottom and the right edge.
  • Check the words before you burn: names and slang are where speech recognition fails.

FAQ

What are karaoke style captions?

Captions where each word changes colour exactly when it is spoken. On short videos they are usually combined with word-by-word captions (1–3 words at a time).

How do I make karaoke subtitles for free?

Whisper word timestamps → ASS file with a \k tag per word → burn in with FFmpeg. Or time it by hand in Aegisub.

Can Premiere Pro highlight the spoken word?

Not on its own – caption tracks use one style. Use a plugin, keyframe the highlight by hand, or burn the captions in first.

\k or \kf?

\k switches the whole word at once (TikTok look), \kf fills it from left to right (sing-along look).

Does WordCaps Studio work on Mac?

Not yet – Windows 10 and 11 only. On Mac, the free ASS + FFmpeg way works.
