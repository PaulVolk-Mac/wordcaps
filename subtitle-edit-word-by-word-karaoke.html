Subtitle Edit: word-by-word and karaoke highlight captions

Subtitle Edit is a free, open-source subtitle editor. Recent versions (5.x, current 5.4) transcribe on your own PC and can turn the result into the fast word-by-word or karaoke-highlight captions you see on TikTok and Shorts. This guide covers the full path: word timing, the effect, and getting it into a video.

Step 1: Get word-level timing

A karaoke highlight only looks right if every word has its own start and end time. With sentence-level subtitles the highlight drifts.

  • Open your video in Subtitle Edit and use Audio to text (speech-to-text with Whisper, runs offline after the model download).
  • Pick the Purfview Faster-Whisper-XXL engine and open Advanced speech-to-text parameters. Its presets include Single words (one word per line) and Highlight word (the whole line with the spoken word marked).
  • Already have a subtitle? Split subtitle and Break/split long lines make lines shorter, but the word times are then estimated, not measured.
  • Check the text before styling. Fixing names and wrong words is much more work after the effect is applied.

Step 2: Switch to ASS and pick an effect

The effects use ASS styling tags, so first set the format to Advanced Sub Station Alpha (.ass). Then open ASSA tools → Apply advanced effects…. Effects in version 5.4 (names as in the menu):

  • Word by word – reveals the text one word at a time.
  • Karaoke – highlights the characters in sequence, left to right, like lyrics.
  • Karaoke - active word pop – needs lines with the active word marked (for example from the Highlight word preset); dims the other words and gives the active word a pop-glow.
  • Smooth fill – a highlight colour sweeps through each word as it is spoken.
  • Bold captions – thick outline, the spoken word lights up in colour and grows. This is the closest to the TikTok look.
  • Blur-in words, Typewriter, Rolling captions – more styles for other formats.

Set font, size and position under ASSA tools → Styles…. For vertical video, use ASSA tools → Change resolution… with 1080×1920 so sizes and positions match the video.

Step 3: Burn the captions into the video

Most editors (CapCut included) and platforms only read the plain text of a subtitle file and drop the ASS effects. To keep the look, burn it in: Video → Generate video with burned-in subtitles…. The result is a normal MP4 you can upload or put into any editor.

If you would rather keep editing the captions in CapCut, export SubRip (.srt) and import it there: how to import an SRT file into CapCut. The highlight effect is lost then, and CapCut's own caption styles take over.

Common problems

The highlight is ahead of or behind the voice

  • The word times were estimated (split lines) instead of measured. Run Audio to text again with a word-level preset.
  • The subtitle was made from a different cut. Transcribe the final edit.

The effect does nothing or the menu is missing

The format is still SubRip. Switch to Advanced Sub Station Alpha first.

Text is tiny or off-screen in a vertical video

The ASS script resolution does not match the video. Use ASSA tools → Change resolution….

Burning in takes long

The burn-in re-encodes the whole video. Check the look on a short test clip first.

FAQ

Can Subtitle Edit make word-by-word captions?

Yes. Audio to text with the Faster-Whisper-XXL presets Single words or Highlight word, then an effect under ASSA tools.

Where is the karaoke effect?

Set the format to Advanced Sub Station Alpha, then ASSA tools → Apply advanced effects… → Karaoke, Smooth fill or Bold captions.

Why do the effects disappear in CapCut or on YouTube?

They are ASS tags that most players and editors ignore. Burn the subtitles into the video first.

Is Subtitle Edit free?

Yes, free and open source.
