What is the TikTok safe zone?

The part of a 9:16 video that the TikTok app does not cover. TikTok’s official safe zone file for in-feed ads (checked 19 Nov 2026) keeps 240 px free at the top, 660 px at the bottom and 120 px on each side at 1080×1920, plus a 180 px button column on the right in the lower half. Organic posts have no button below the caption, so slightly more room at the bottom is usually visible.

Are Reels and Shorts different?

Yes. Google’s official overlay for vertical YouTube ads keeps 288 px free at the top, 672 px at the bottom, 192 px on the right and 48 px on the left. Meta officially asks to keep the bottom 35% (672 px) of a Reels ad free; the top and side values shown here for Reels are our estimates. “All-in-one” uses the strictest margin of all three, so one edit works everywhere.

How does the “text in covered area” warning work?

The checker looks at the pixels of the frame you see: spots with many hard light/dark edges in both directions (typical for captions and logos) inside a covered zone are marked yellow. It is a hint, not text recognition – busy backgrounds can trigger it, and soft low-contrast text can be missed. “Check the whole video” tests two frames per second and lists the moments with a warning.

Where do the numbers come from?

From the official help pages for advertisers: TikTok’s downloadable in-feed safe zone file, Google’s vertical video ad overlay and Meta’s Reels ads documentation. Each platform tab shows its source link and the date we last checked it; the same data is in zones.json. We could not verify an official safe zone for Instagram Stories, so Stories are not included.

Is my video uploaded?

No. Your file is drawn on a canvas inside this page and never leaves your computer. You can use the checker offline once it has loaded.

How do I use the PNG overlay in CapCut?

Add the PNG as an overlay on the top track over the whole clip, place captions and faces inside the green box, then delete or hide the overlay before you export.

Where should captions go?

Inside the green box, ideally in its lower third – high enough to clear the caption and music line, low enough not to cover faces.
