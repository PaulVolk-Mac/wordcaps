4-week training plan

How to progress (double progression): stay in the rep range. When you reach the top of the range in all sets, add weight next time (the step is shown under each exercise) and start again at the bottom of the range.

    Want to track it? The Workout Planner & Tracker (Excel / Google Sheets) logs every set, shows your next target per exercise and your personal records.

    How is the plan built?

    2 days = full body A/B, 3 days = full body (beginners alternate A-B-A / B-A-B), 4 days = upper/lower, 5 days = push/pull/legs + upper/lower, 6 days = push/pull/legs twice. Every week trains a squat or lunge, a hinge, a push and a pull. The first big lift of each day gets the most sets and the longest rest.

    Why a deload in week 4?

    Weeks 1–3 get harder: about 3, 2, then 1 rep left in the tank. Week 4 halves the sets at about 90 % of the weight so your joints and energy recover. Then start again at week 1 with the weights you reached.
