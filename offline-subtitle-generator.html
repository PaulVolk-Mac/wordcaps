Best offline subtitle generators for Windows (free & paid)

Most “auto subtitle” websites want you to upload your video first. That is slow for big files, needs an account, and your footage sits on someone else's server. An offline subtitle generator does the speech recognition on your own PC instead. Today almost all of them use the same open AI model – Whisper – so the real differences are price, effort and what the result looks like.

All tools in the table run the speech recognition locally (some download a model once). Prices and features change – check the makers' own pages. As of November 2026.

1. Subtitle Edit – best free tool for correct subtitle files

Subtitle Edit (Windows, free) has an “audio to text” function that runs Whisper on your PC. You get timed subtitles you can fix line by line, check against the waveform and save as SRT, ASS, VTT and many more formats. Best for: accurate subtitles for YouTube, films or translations. Not for: the animated one-word-at-a-time look of Shorts and TikToks.

2. Buzz – the simplest free Whisper app

Buzz is an open-source (MIT) desktop app for Windows, macOS and Linux. Open an audio or video file, pick a Whisper model, and export TXT, SRT or VTT. It can also transcribe live from the microphone. The Windows build is not code-signed, so expect a SmartScreen warning when installing from the official GitHub release. Best for: quick transcripts and plain subtitle files.

3. Kdenlive – free editor with built-in speech recognition

The free video editor Kdenlive can create a subtitle track with Whisper or VOSK directly on the timeline. It needs a Python environment and model download the first time, and recent versions moved the settings around, so set-up takes some patience. Best for: people who already edit in Kdenlive.

4. Whisper + FFmpeg – free, full control

Run whisper or faster-whisper with word timestamps, write an ASS file with karaoke tags and burn it in with ffmpeg -vf ass=captions.ass. That gives you exactly the TikTok-style look for free – after some scripting. Step by step in How to make word-by-word captions and Karaoke style captions.

5. Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve Studio – if you already pay for them

Premiere Pro's Speech to Text works offline once the language pack is installed (Adobe added this in version 22.2). DaVinci Resolve's automatic subtitles are part of the paid Studio version and also run on your machine. Both create a normal captions track; neither highlights the active word out of the box. Details: Premiere Pro word-by-word captions, DaVinci Resolve (free version) word-by-word subtitles.

6. WordCaps Studio – offline captions with the short-form look

WordCaps Studio is a small Windows app built for one job: animated, word-by-word captions for Shorts, TikToks and Reels, made entirely offline.

  1. Drop one or several videos into the window – batch mode handles a whole folder.
  2. Pick a look (classic, pop, box, minimal, bounce, karaoke, single word, neon), colour, font and size.
  3. Check the recognised words in the live preview and fix names.
  4. Press Burn: you get the finished MP4 plus SRT and ASS files for CapCut, Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve.

Your video never leaves the PC: no account, no upload, no watermark, no subscription. Works on Windows 10/11 with any CPU; an NVIDIA GPU is optional and makes it faster. Recognises English, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese and more automatically.

7. UIDCaption and MS Auto Captions – other Windows apps with the animated look

WordCaps Studio is not the only small Windows app for this job. UIDCaption (launched in autumn 2026) transcribes locally in over 90 languages, highlights the spoken word and exports the video with captions baked in; its maker lists a lifetime licence for $39. MS Auto Captions burns word-by-word captions into the video with 11 preset animation styles, offers an offline transcription mode and is sold as a one-time purchase with a licence key (internet is needed once for activation). Both are worth a look if you want their particular styles. WordCaps Studio is the low-price option of the three at €4.99 once, with batch mode and SRT/ASS export included.

Why offline at all?

  • Privacy: client footage, unreleased content or family videos stay on your disk.
  • Speed with big files: no waiting for a 2 GB 4K upload before anything happens.
  • No limits: no minutes quota, no monthly plan, no watermark on the free tier.
  • Works anywhere: on the train, on slow hotel Wi-Fi, or with no internet at all.

When an online tool is the better choice

If you edit on a phone or a Mac, want automatic emojis, B-roll or AI hook titles, or work in a team, cloud tools such as CapCut, Submagic or Captions do more. If you want clean subtitles or animated captions on your own Windows PC without uploading anything, one of the offline tools above is enough.

FAQ

What is the best free offline subtitle generator?

For accurate SRT files: Subtitle Edit or Buzz, both free and both using Whisper on your PC. For animated word-by-word captions for free: Whisper + an ASS karaoke file + FFmpeg. WordCaps Studio (€4.99 once) does the animated version in one click; UIDCaption and MS Auto Captions are paid alternatives.

Is there an offline AI subtitle generator?

Yes – Whisper is an open AI speech model that runs locally. Subtitle Edit, Buzz, Kdenlive and WordCaps Studio all use it; after the one-time model download no internet is needed.

Can I generate subtitles without uploading my video?

Yes. Every tool on this page processes the audio on your own computer. Browser tools (CapCut online, Submagic, Veed and similar) upload the video.

Which one burns animated captions into the video?

WordCaps Studio (€4.99 once), UIDCaption ($39 once) and MS Auto Captions (one-time purchase) each do it in one step. The free route is Whisper word timestamps + ASS karaoke tags + FFmpeg.

Do I need a good graphics card?

No. Whisper runs on a normal CPU. An NVIDIA GPU makes the larger, more accurate models much faster.
