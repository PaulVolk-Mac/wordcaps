Auto captions for Instagram Reels – 4 ways, from the free sticker to word-by-word

Instagram can caption a Reel for you, and for many videos that is enough. If you want the bold captions where every spoken word lights up, you have to burn them into the video before you upload. Here are four ways, starting with the free ones.

1. Instagram's Captions sticker (free, built in)

  1. Record or upload your clip in the Reels editor.
  2. Open the sticker tray and choose Captions. Instagram transcribes the speech, which takes a few seconds.
  3. Tap the captions to open the text, then tap a word to correct it.
  4. Pick one of the styles, change the colour and drag the captions where you want them.

Meta's separate video editor Edits also has auto captions and exports straight to Instagram.

Good: free, no extra app, done in a minute. Limits: only a few looks, the text can only be made inside the app, and the sticker is not offered in every language or region. If the sticker is missing, update the app and check whether your language is supported.

Viewers can also switch on automatic closed captions for videos themselves. Those are plain subtitles that you cannot style, and most people never turn them on.

2. Free, technical: Whisper + FFmpeg

The open-source speech model Whisper gives you a timestamp for every word (for example faster-whisper with word_timestamps=True). Write the words into an ASS subtitle file, one event per word or with karaoke tags, and burn it in: ffmpeg -i reel.mp4 -vf ass=captions.ass out.mp4. The details are in How to make word-by-word captions and Karaoke style captions.

Cost: 0 €. Time: an evening of setup the first time; Python knowledge helps.

3. Editor apps with auto captions

Many phone and desktop editors caption a Reel in one tap. Check what the free plan really includes: CapCut moved auto captions into its Pro plan in 2025 for most users, and other free editors add a watermark or limit exports. Online tools also need you to upload the video. See CapCut auto captions without Pro and free and offline Submagic alternatives.

Tips for Reels captions

  • Keep them in the middle. The bottom of a Reel is covered by your account name, the caption text and the audio line, the right edge by the like, comment and share buttons. Captions in the middle of the frame, or slightly below it, are safe.
  • 2–3 words on screen read fastest on a phone. One word at a time suits fast, punchy talk.
  • Check names and numbers. Speech recognition gets these wrong most often, whichever tool you use.
  • One file for every platform. Burned-in captions are part of the picture, so the same MP4 works on Facebook Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts (see captions for YouTube Shorts).

FAQ

Does Instagram have auto captions for Reels?

Yes. The Captions sticker in the Reels editor transcribes the speech and puts the text on the video. You can correct words and change style, colour and position. The choice of looks is small, and availability depends on your language and app version.

Can you edit auto captions on Instagram Reels?

Yes. Tap the captions in the editor, then tap a word to correct it. Check names, brand words and numbers.

How do I get word-by-word captions with a highlighted word?

Burn them in before you upload: free with Whisper + FFmpeg, in a paid editor such as CapCut Pro, or offline on Windows with WordCaps Studio for €12.99 once.

Do the same captions work on Facebook Reels?

Yes. Burned-in captions are part of the video, so the same file works on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube Shorts.
